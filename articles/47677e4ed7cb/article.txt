Receipts Before Mythology

Every mystery becomes dangerous at the same moment,

when the story becomes easier to repeat than the evidence is to check.

CAW has lived inside that danger for years.

Some saw a forgotten branch of SHIB. Others saw coincidence, a scam, a riddle, a revolution, an unfinished experiment, or the return of someone who had already disappeared.

The strange part is not that the trail was hidden.

The strange part is how much of it was visible all along.

Ethereum does not remember who sounded convincing, who had the largest audience, or which version of the story became popular. It remembers transactions, block numbers, contract code, calldata, receipts, logs, and execution results.

That is where this origin story begins.

Not with Ryoshi.

Not with belief.

With the receipts.

The Five Labels

Every important claim in this article will be marked according to what the evidence actually supports.

FACT — Directly observed or deterministically reproduced evidence: a transaction, receipt, raw log, runtime hash, storage value, block timestamp, or locally reproduced artifact.

INFERENCE — A reasoned interpretation supported by facts but not directly written on-chain.

IDEA — A proposed explanation, comparison, or experiment that has not yet been proven.

UNKNOWN — A question the available evidence does not resolve.

STOP CONDITION — The point beyond which a stronger claim would exceed the evidence.

Four Questions That Must Never Be Collapsed

The CAW origin story repeatedly crosses four different questions.

1. What happened on-chain?

A transaction can prove that an address transferred ETH, deployed a contract, approved a router, added or removed liquidity, submitted calldata, emitted an event, or funded another address.

This is the strongest layer.

It describes activity.

2. Who controlled the wallet?

A valid transaction proves that the required signing authority was used at that moment.

It does not prove whether the key belonged to one person, several people, a custodian, an automated system, a compromised operator, or someone who acquired the key later.

Wallet activity is evidence of key use.

It is not a permanent biography of the keyholder.

3. Who authored the pseudonymous work?

A wallet can deploy a contract. A Medium account can publish an essay. A GitHub account can push code. A Telegram account can post a message. An image can contain a riddle.

Those records may form a pattern, but they do not automatically prove one author controlled every surface.

Writing style, repeated phrases, misspellings, symbols, or philosophy may support an inference.

They do not establish authorship by themselves.

4. Who is the human being?

A blockchain address is not a legal identity.

An explorer label is not a passport.

A profile name is not cryptographic proof.

Even a valid signature proves control of a key, not necessarily the legal identity of the signer.

This article investigates the CAW origin trail.

It does not claim to identify Ryoshi.

The chain can prove a path.

It cannot name the person walking it unless additional proof is supplied.

Why ERC-1155 Requires a Separate Scanner

ERC-20 normally represents one token movement per Transfer event.

ERC-1155 may represent either one movement or many movements inside one event.

The two ERC-1155 event types are:

TransferSingle
TransferBatch

Both contain three indexed address roles:

operator
from
to

The canonical scan therefore requires six independent query families:

TransferSingle operator
TransferSingle from
TransferSingle to

TransferBatch operator
TransferBatch from
TransferBatch to

This distinction prevents a serious attribution error.

The ERC-1155 event field from identifies the balance holder whose token balance decreased.

It does not automatically identify the transaction signer.

Likewise, an operator appearance does not automatically mean the Shiba Inu Deployer wallet B8F sent or received the token being moved.

STOP CONDITION — Do not classify an operator-only appearance as a balance movement involving the Shiba Inu Deployer wallet B8F.

Canonical ERC-1155 Identity

Each expanded movement therefore requires an additional field:

Without batchIndex, multiple token movements emitted by one batch log would be incorrectly collapsed into one record.
