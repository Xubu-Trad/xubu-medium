They’re not.

The riddles are Caw’s manifest.. 
the part that can be traced, reproduced, and checked..

The “manifesto” is one artifact in that trail, 
a layer surfaced through the first puzzle.

They’re linked, but they serve different purposes.

Here are the hard anchors you can verify without trusting anyone..

May 9, 2022: the CAW deployer address 0xbaeEDcCcbFB112d4a921dAa635AC2276307A1705 
sent a self-transfer of 0.666 ETH with input that decodes to “58bZfQ1” 
— a slug that leads to the first image clue.

July 17, 2022: the same deployer address sent a self-transfer of 0.999 ETH with input that decodes to “zrUfKaKV”

That’s the difference..

meaning can be argued, evidence can be reproduced.

Inside the CAW Riddles..

A Forensic Reconstruction..

Origin of the Hunt-

Every story leaves footprints.

CAW’s trace begins with a funding chain linking back to Shiba Inu (SHIB)’s creator.

On June 15 2021, the SHIB deployer wallet sent 2.78 ETH to an intermediary address.

303 days of silence followed…

Then on April 14 2022, that intermediary forwarded 2.22 ETH into a fresh CAW deployer address. Just 2 minutes and 20 seconds later, that very address deployed the CAW token contract, renouncing control immediately after.

[Image: 1*u9hV77QEqjsiT9Q7dQU7rw.png]

Table: Verifiable funding chain of CAW. The CAW deployer (funded indirectly by SHIB’s origin) created the token contract within minutes of receiving funds.

The flow was deliberate..

This on-chain receipt trail was public for anyone to see — 
SHIB → intermediary → CAW deployer → CAW.

Such a clean lineage is not coincidence, it’s the puzzle’s first clue.

On-chain, what stands out is the transaction structure more than any personality or story, there’s an intermediary step in the flow that creates separation at a glance, but it still leaves a clean, followable trail for anyone willing to trace it end-to-end. That’s a factual pattern — indirection plus traceability — and it matters because it’s the difference between “a rumor you can argue about” and “a chain of receipts you can verify.” Pair that with the timing and the unusually deliberate-looking ETH values, and the most defensible way to frame it is simple, the trail was engineered to be missed by casual observers but recoverable by serious hunters —

a breadcrumb path that doesn’t require trust, only verification.

Equally telling is how CAW actually launched, not a hush-hush whitelist “presale,” but a public CopperLaunch / Balancer V2 Liquidity Bootstrapping Pool that ran April 14–17, 2022, seeded with about ~466.67T CAW and 1.8 ETH — meaning price discovery and early distribution happened in the open, on-chain, where anyone could show up and buy, and everyone could audit the flow afterward.

What’s still unusual (and rare) is what the contract doesn’t appear to give its deployer, in the captured probes, the proxy admin slot and implementation slot read as 0x0, and delegatecall is flagged false, which cuts against the common “we’ll upgrade later” control surface that so many launches quietly keep in their back pocket.

Plus the deployer’s behavior around launch looks consistent with a “deploy-and-step-back” posture — no recorded pre-launch activity, then funding and deployment actions clustered around launch day, more like a one-time ignition than an operator gearing up to manage a project.

and then came the riddles…

The mysterious CAW token deployer wallet (0xbaeEDcCcbFB112d4a921dAa635AC2276307A1705) embedded the starting keys for two elaborate riddles directly onto the Ethereum blockchain. Each clue came in the form of a self-addressed transaction with symbolic values and a short data payload:

Transaction 1 — May 9, 2022: TXID

[Image: 0*76yiFJqtVbU6-ybh]

https://etherscan.io/tx/0xfbbcf5338b4a9c35073ac7253afc1a8ee81770d8d3285b80497bbd9c2186ed5b

0xfbbcf5338b4a9c35073ac7253afc1a8ee81770d8d3285b80497bbd9c2186ed5b, from the deployer to itself carrying 0.666 ETH (with gas deliberately ~66.67% consumed) and input data 58bZfQ1. This “666” motif (two-thirds gas = 66.67%) nods to the “number of the beast” and inversion theme – indeed 0.999 in the next clue is 0.666 upside-down.

Transaction 2 — July 17, 2022: TXID

[Image: 1*vsy9KRZ2gLTAORFJItN9XQ.png]

https://etherscan.io/tx/0xcae4b15350b3ccc2b37fec5caa718560241ec181bc49741d5d1199d1d32412d4

0xcae4b15350b3ccc2b37fec5caa718560241ec181bc49741d5d1199d1d32412d4, again self-sent by the deployer with 0.999 ETH (also ~66.67% gas used) and input zrUfKaKV. As expected, 999 mirrors 666, reinforcing a duality theme.

same signature
same author

Both transactions’ full hashes, values, and payloads were public on-chain – an immutable trailhead proclaiming the riddles’ legitimacy (coming from the token’s creator) and inviting the cawmmunity to investigate.

so we did…

and each hex data string turned out to be a key for an external resource.

Interpreted as UTF-8, 58bZfQ1 and zrUfKaKV weren’t random gibberish but rather slugs/IDs for content on the web.

In fact, the deployer was effectively saying “go to these addresses.” 
The first pointed to an image link; the second to a Pastebin page.

[Image: 1*2ZTKI0Pe2VItKYV7HXM3HQ.png]

Step by Step

58bZfQ1 aka Riddle 1 (R1):

“Epic of Gilgamesh” Image — Hidden Poems, Coords, the Manifesto, and more

Transaction 1 — the real payload was in the Input Data field,
 an alphanumeric string 58bZfQ1. No explanation, just that string.

The community’s first test had arrived.

Within hours the CAW community proved its mettle. Telegram handle @KimDamyun ( a member of the cawmmunity and og riddle hunter in 2022) recognized 58bZfQ1 as a possible ImgBB image code (the format looks random but is short, fitting an image ID). Sure enough, visiting ibb.co/58bZfQ1 revealed an image – the first riddle image…

[Image: 0*xNj8KcOHfU_bBWPj]

Proposalforadecentralizedcoupfortehpplbytehppl

[Image: 1*awpPNTJm63dVbP4ti1aXEQ.png]

This image itself contained more hidden content.

Cawmmunity members quickly discovered that the 7-character string from the 0.666 ETH transaction fit at the end of an Imgbb URL. Indeed, 58bZfQ1 led to an image on ibb.co, https://ibb.co/58bZfQ1, titled..

“Proposal for a decentralized coup for teh ppl by teh ppl”.

This picture (640×776 PNG) showed a photograph of the Yale tablet of the Epic of Gilgamesh – a clear hint at ancient Mesopotamian lore

By 1:30 AM the next day (May 10), hunters had found the original image here:

https://www.gutenberg.org/files/11000/11000-h/11000-h.htm

then noticed there was a copy of the image super imposed on top and a secret message embedded steganographically in that image.

that secret message was the word “The” visible in one corner. The odd overlay of the word “The” suggested the image itself contained an embedded message.

GLOSSARY OF NAMES 

A SHORT description of the gods and of other persons and places mentioned in the Epic will be found in this 
Glossary. The gods were credited at different times with a variety of attributes and characteristics, sometimes 
contradictory; only such as are relevant to the material of the Gilgamesh Epic are given here. The small number of gods 
and other characters who play a more important part in the story are described in the introduction; in their case a page 
reference to this description is given at the end of the Glossary note. Cross-references to other entries in the Glossary are 
indicated by means of italics. 

AD AD: Storm-, rain-, and weather-god. 

ANUNNAKI; Usually gods of the underworld, judges of the dead and offspring of Anu. 

ANSHAN: A district of Elam in south-west Persia; probably the source o£ supplies of wood for making bows. 
Gilgamesh has a bow of Anshan'. 

ANTUM: Wife of Anu. 

ANU: Sumerian An; father of gods, and god of the firmament, the 'great above'. In the Sumerian cosmogony there 
was, first of all, the primeval sea, from which was born the cosmic mountain consisting of heaven, 'An', and earth, 'Ki'; 
they were separated by Enlil, then An carried off the heavens, and Enlil the earth. Ann later retreated more and more into 
the background; he had an important temple in Umk. 

APSU: The Abyss; the primeval waters under the earth; in the later mythology of the Enuma Elish, more 
particularly the sweet water which mingled with the bitter waters of the sea and with a third watery element, perhaps 
cloud, from which the first gods were engendered. The waters of Apsu were thought of as held immobile underground by 
the 'spell' of Ea in a death-like sleep. 

ARURU: A goddess of creation, she created Enkidu from clay in the image of Anu. 

AY A: The dawn, the bride of the Sun God Shamash. 

BELIT-SHER1: Scribe and recorder of the underworld gods: 

BULL of HEAVEN: A personification of drought created by Anu for Ishtar. 

DILMUN: The Sumerian paradise, perhaps the Persian Gulf; sometimes described as 'the place where the sun rises' 
and 'the Land of the Living'; the scene of a Sumerian creation myth and the place where the deified Sumerian hero of the 
flood, Ziusudra, was taken by the gods to live for ever. See p. 39. 

DUMUZI: The Sumerian form of Tammuz; a god of vegetation and fertility, and so of the underworld, also called 
'the Shepherd and 'lord of the sheepfolds'. As the companion of Ningizzida 'to all eternity' he stands at the gate of heaven. 
In the Sumerian 'Descent of Inanna' he is the husband of the goddess Inanna, the Sumerian counterpart of Ishtar. 
According to the Sumerian King-List Gilgamesh was descended from 'Dumuzi a shepherd'. 

EA: Sumerian Enki; god of the sweet waters, also of wisdom, a patron of arts and one of the creators of mankind, 
towards whom he is usually well-disposed. The chief god of Eridu, where he had a temple, he lived 'in the deep'; his 
ancestry is uncertain, but he was probably a child of Anu. 

EANNA: The temple precinct in Urtik sacred to Anu and Ishtar. 

EGALMAH: The 'Great Palace' in Uruk, the home of the goddess Ninsun, the mother of Gilgamesh. 

ENDUSUGGA: With Nindukugga, Sumerian gods living in the underworld; parents of Enlil. 

ENKIDU: Moulded by Arum, goddess of creation, out of clay is the image and 'of the essence of Anu', the sky-god, 
and of Ninurta the war-god. The companion of Gilgamesh, he is wild or natural reran; he was later considered a patron or 
god of anima b and may have been the hero of another cycle. See P. 30. 

ENLIL: God of earth, wind, and the universal air, ultimately spirit; the executive of Anu. In the Sumerian 
cosmogony he was born of the union of An heaven, and Ki earth. These he separated, and he then carried off earth as his 
portion. In later times he supplanted Anu as chief god. He was the patron of the city of Nippur. See p. 24. 

ENMUL: See Endukugga. 

ENNUGI: God of irrigation and inspector of Canals. 

ENUMA ELLISH: The Semitic creation epic which describes the creation of the gods, the defeat of the powers of 
chaos by the young god Marduk, and the creation of man from the blood of Kingu, the defeated champion of chaos. 
The title is taken from the first words of the epic 'When on high'. 

ERESHKIGAL: The Queen of the underworld, a counterpart of Persephone; probably once a sky-goddess. In 
the Sumerian cosmogony she was carried off to the underworld after the separation of heaven and earth. See p. 27. 

ETANA: Legendary king of Kish who reigned after the flood; in the epic which bears his name he was carried 
to heaven on the back of an eagle.

Importantly, by taking the first letter of each word (and converting any spelled-out numbers into digits), the acrostic spelled out an IPFS Content Identifier (CID):

the image also yielded an IPFS hash:

QmddMfUi8AgsRyqa8MdsWqoCLYmV6kVJ4PYm6uo3iQ7WCV

An IPFS hash is a content-addressed identifier — in this case pointing to a file stored on the decentralized IPFS network. When resolvers fetched that hash, it downloaded a file: specifically, a .ape audio file (Monkey’s Audio format).

in 2022 the hunters were able to download the .ape file and recorded it as such,,,

[Image: 1*VeSmEueetcmsIn17pItU1A.png]

however in my runs to replicate the same file in 2025/26' i didn’t replicate the .ape layer end-to-end in a forensic, “anyone can reproduce it from scratch” sense because the chain broke at the deterministic CID derivation step.

[Image: 1*V2rAMWYKrs0Cio6wPC07Qg.png]

At the same time, we did record the CID and even a local payload path for the .ape, but we didn’t lock it down as a single canonical binary with sha256 + exact extraction/fetch commands published in the pack/repo — so we couldn’t honestly call it “replicated” under strict chain-of-custody standards. but the hunters in 2022 recorded their findings to provide us with that insight.

Fetching the data behind that CID yielded a packaged payload (the solver downloaded a small ZIP file). 
Inside were two files:

first_riddle_song.ape and first_riddle.txt.

The .ape file name “Tre rebus musicali” (Italian for “Three musical riddles”) was itself a clue. An .ape is an audio file (Monkey’s Audio format), suggesting the next layer was audio steganography.

Sure enough, using the tool DeepSound (which can hide files in audio) to inspect first_riddle_song.ape revealed it was concealing an embedded text file – but required a password to extract. The hidden file was named enkidu.txt, which hints at the password: “only the king’s lover has ears to hear” – in the Epic, Enkidu is the close companion of Gilgamesh. Using “enkidu” as the password, solvers unlocked the hidden text file via DeepSound.

[Image: 1*4SkyZW3rNT0MSvxms7ALhQ.png]

Inside enkidu.txt was yet another encoded string – a lengthy hex sequence, but curiously it contained letters beyond the usual 0-9 and a-f. In fact, letters U through Z appeared in place of A–F, evidently a simple substitution cipher on the hex digits. Once the team mapped U→A, V→B, … Z→F and then heeded the poem’s last advice (“walk backwards”) by reversing this hex string, the result could be decoded as ASCII and then themanifesto was born...

Pastebin Content — Hex-Encoded Binary..

The restored Pastebin did not contain a neat poem or list. 2884 hex characters → 1442 bytes (plus a trailing newline in the raw scrape)

That’s important because it means: we’re not debating interpretations yet — we’re still in byte-land.

This solved what the community had debated since 2022: the second riddle was not another EXIF cipher or audio embed. It was a linguistic test… a hidden message disguised as hex, meant to be translated from emotion, not from bytes.

or so it seemed..

Peter’s ZRU “findings” are only “canon” in the forensic sense, they’re things we can reproduce and re-test, not a completed answer or final solution.

Two parts are high-confidence (0.9) because they repeat cleanly.

First, Peter’s long hex string (the one starting 787e2963...) is treated as the official input blob we’re working from, and we added checks to make sure we’re using the exact same starting bytes—so we don’t get into copy/paste drift arguments.

Second, his note that you can unhex the data and then decode it as UTF-16 and get something that looks like “maybe a poem” is also solid as an observation: when we did it, the output matched

but…

What we don’t want to overstate (medium confidence, ~0.7) is what that output means. A block of text that looks poem-like or even CJK-ish isn’t proof there’s real meaning there. If you take random-ish bytes and interpret them as UTF-16/UTF-32, you can get something that looks like language even when it’s basically noise — and we call that out in the replication notes.

We’ve found bits of data that look meaningful — stuff that resembles QR codes, fragments that resemble a URL, and other patterns that seem like they could be “something.” But so far, none of it has turned into a clear, repeatable breakthrough.

In the end, the zrUfKaKV riddle is still unsolved.
