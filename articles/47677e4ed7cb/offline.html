Four Questions That Must Never Be Collapsed

The CAW origin story repeatedly crosses four different questions.

1. What happened on-chain?

A transaction can prove that an address transferred ETH, deployed a contract, approved a router, added or removed liquidity, submitted calldata, emitted an event, or funded another address.

This is the strongest layer.

It describes activity.

2. Who controlled the wallet?

A valid transaction proves that the required signing authority was used at that moment.

It does not prove whether the key belonged to one person, several people, a custodian, an automated system, a compromised operator, or someone who acquired the key later.

Wallet activity is evidence of key use.

It is not a permanent biography of the keyholder.

3. Who authored the pseudonymous work?

A wallet can deploy a contract. A Medium account can publish an essay. A GitHub account can push code. A Telegram account can post a message. An image can contain a riddle.

Those records may form a pattern, but they do not automatically prove one author controlled every surface.

Writing style, repeated phrases, misspellings, symbols, or philosophy may support an inference.

They do not establish authorship by themselves.

4. Who is the human being?

A blockchain address is not a legal identity.

An explorer label is not a passport.

A profile name is not cryptographic proof.

Even a valid signature proves control of a key, not necessarily the legal identity of the signer.

This article investigates the CAW origin trail.

It does not claim to identify Ryoshi.

The chain can prove a path.

It cannot name the person walking it unless additional proof is supplied.
